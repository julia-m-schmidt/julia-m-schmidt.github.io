### Data, Software, and Credit: Bank Lending Constraints and Digital Investment in Europe

<p class="paper-authors">With <a href="https://sites.google.com/view/lisepatureau/">Lise Patureau</a>, <a href="https://sites.google.com/site/fabientripier/home">Fabien Tripier</a>, Marine Charlotte André and Julie Delanote</p>

<div class="paper-links">
  <a href="/assets/docs/P1_AI_Firm_Finance_SchmidtJ.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Slides</a>
</div>

<p class="paper-summary">Using linked EIBIS, Orbis and BankFocus data for European firms, we study how financing conditions shape investment in software and databases and the associated productivity gains. Data-related investment is associated with higher labour productivity and TFPR, while bank credit constraints bind more clearly for tangible investment.</p>

### Two Faces of Data: Productivity and Markups in the Digital Age

<p class="paper-authors">With <a href="https://www.isaacbaley.com/">Isaac Baley</a> and <a href="https://sites.google.com/view/alejandrorabano/">Alejandro Rábano</a></p>

<div class="paper-links">
  <a href="/assets/docs/P2_Two_Faces_Data_SchmidtJ.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Slides</a>
</div>

<p class="paper-summary">We study whether data widens firm inequality through productivity, market power, or both. A model with separate innovation and marketing data is paired with French firm-level evidence: production data are associated with higher TFPR, while consumer data are associated with higher markups.</p>

### The Carbon Cost of Compute: AI, Data Externalities, and Climate Policy

<p class="paper-authors">Solo-authored</p>

<p class="paper-summary">We develop a dynamic general-equilibrium model in which carbon-intensive compute and unpriced household-generated data produce AI services. AI can raise emissions before delivering economic gains, while carbon pricing still cuts emissions at modest output cost. The welfare-maximizing carbon tax lies below the social cost of carbon, with market power explaining most of the gap and the data externality playing a smaller role.</p>
